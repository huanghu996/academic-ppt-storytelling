# Scientific Argument: Give Every Claim an Appropriate Basis

Use when establishing the argument or reviewing results, discussion, and literature slides. An evidence ledger organizes verification; it does not replace reading the source.

## 1. Make the claim inspectable

State the object, condition, baseline, and change. Then identify the kind of claim.

| Type | What it may say | Required basis |
|---|---|---|
| Observation | A relationship appears in the inspected records | Data, images, records, or a precise source location |
| Interpretation | A mechanism is consistent with the observation | Controls, theory, or literature, with competing explanations acknowledged |
| Hypothesis | Changing a factor is expected to affect an outcome | Rationale and a falsifiable condition, clearly unverified |
| Causal claim | Changing a factor produces an outcome under stated identification assumptions | Domain-appropriate intervention, identification, or mechanistic evidence; correlation alone is insufficient |
| Plan | A specified test will examine the hypothesis | Variables, controls, criteria, and feasibility |
| Theoretical conclusion | A result follows under specified assumptions | Assumptions, the relevant derivation or proof, and its scope |

"No improvement was observed in this test" does not establish universal ineffectiveness. A difference failing a significance threshold does not by itself establish equivalence. Use descriptive wording when stronger inference is unavailable.

## 2. Claim–evidence ledger

Use a compact table for a full talk and short notes for a small revision. Assign stable claim IDs, such as C01, and evidence IDs, such as E01.

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Claim ID and exact wording | The sentence intended for a title, annotation, or conclusion |
| Type and attribution | Observation, hypothesis, etc.; original authors, current study, or presenter's assessment |
| Evidence ID and status | Inspected, supplied but unchecked, missing, or illustrative |
| Source location | File and figure/table/page/variable, or DOI/URL and section; include version/date |
| Comparison conditions | Sampling unit, cases, training/test purpose, baseline, metric, and transformations |
| Support scope | The precise assertion and conditions covered by this item |
| Uncertainty and limitations | Meaning of intervals, omitted variability, alternative explanations, and extrapolation limits |
| Slide use | Slides affected if the source or claim changes |

A published figure without raw data may support the comparison shown. It does not permit inventing standard deviations, significance, repeat counts, or performance in unreported conditions. Record those limits explicitly.

## 3. Locate the missing inference

Work backward from conclusion to grounds, connecting reasoning, and boundary:

1. Which evidence directly supports the statement?
2. Why does that evidence answer this question? What assumption connects them?
3. Which competing explanations remain possible?
4. Is there evidence for another object, scale, or condition?

A gap should specify an object or condition. With incomplete literature coverage, say that validation was not found within the inspected scope. Do not turn a limited search into an unsupported priority claim such as "first ever."

## 4. Match the argument to the talk

| Setting | Useful reasoning path | Closing question |
|---|---|---|
| Original research | Question, essential context, hypothesis, test, evidence, boundary | What judgment does this evidence change? |
| Paper discussion | Original question, authors' claims, evidence, dependencies, critique | What does the paper actually establish? |
| Proposal | Question, documented gap, hypothesis, discriminating test, criteria, risks | What outcomes support or challenge the hypothesis? |
| Progress report | Current question, available evidence, remaining explanations, next decision | Which next test is informative? |
| Negative or inconclusive result | Expectation, design, observation, sensitivity, exclusion scope, next test | What does the absence of an observed change mean? |
| Review | Dispute, evidence classes, agreements, disagreements, methodological differences | Why do studies reach different conclusions? |
| Theory | Definitions, assumptions, proposition, key inference, example/counterexample | Which assumptions are necessary? |

## 5. Common inference errors

- Renaming or repartitioning training objects does not establish independent generalization. Verify the actual provenance and split.
- Frames in a time series are not automatically independent replicates. Distinguish records, objects, subjects, cases, and random seeds.
- An interval conditional on fixed records or checkpoints does not automatically cover training randomness, model selection, or unsampled operating conditions.
- A schematic arrow expresses a proposed relationship unless separate evidence establishes it. Distinguish measured and hypothesized links.
- Explain the baseline, denominator, absolute/relative change, normalization, and resource conditions in performance comparisons.
- Distinguish measurement, simulation, reconstruction, and generated illustration. Concept imagery is not observational evidence.

## 6. Progress with incomplete material

Complete the question, supplied-evidence, method-explanation, and planned-test slides. Recast unsupported result pages as questions or plans and mark consequential missing material. Ask for the few inputs that change the conclusion or deliverable, rather than requiring a large intake form before starting.
